At the early stage of Feedple, **privacy policy and terms of service should be simple, transparent, user-friendly, and heavily focused on consent, control, and non-impersonation**. You don’t need 20-page legal documents yet — but you **do** need clear, trustworthy rules that protect you and build user confidence.

### Recommended Approach for Early-Stage Privacy & Terms

#### 1. **Keep It Short & Human-Readable**
   - Aim for 1–2 pages max.
   - Use plain language (no legalese jargon).
   - Example style: Basecamp, Substack, or Notion’s early policies — short, bold headings, bullet points.

#### 2. **Core Principles to Emphasize (Critical for Feedple)**
   Highlight these upfront — they directly counter the risks in the document:

   - **You own your content forever.** We never claim ownership.
   - **Your Personal Companion is trained only on content you publish.** No private data used without explicit opt-in.
   - **Your Companion is not you.** It is an AI aide inspired by your work and always identifies itself as such.
   - **You control everything.** Pause, limit, or delete your Companion at any time.
   - **Explicit consent required.** You decide if your Companion can post, chat, or continue after you're gone.
   - **Post-death legacy is optional.** Requires your prior consent and heir approval.
   - **We do not sell your data.** Ever.

#### 3. **Key Sections to Include**
   - **Privacy Policy** (short):
     - What data we collect (posts, profile info, interactions)
     - How we use it (to train your Companion, improve platform)
     - No selling data, no third-party tracking
     - Deletion = full removal

   - **Terms of Service** (short):
     - You own your content and Companion outputs
     - Companion is a tool, not a person — no sentience claims
     - You’re responsible for what your Companion says (with moderation safeguards)
     - Right to delete or export everything
     - Legacy mode requires explicit opt-in

#### 4. **Add a Dedicated "Personal Companion Agreement"**
   A short, separate checkbox flow when awakening Companion:
   > I understand:
   > - My Companion learns only from my published content
   > - It is an AI aide, not me
   > - I control if it can post or chat
   > - I can delete it anytime
   > - Legacy continuation is optional and requires my consent

   This creates a clear consent trail — very valuable if questions arise later.

#### 5. **Early Stage = Be Conservative & Transparent**
   - Don’t overpromise ("immortal mind," "digital you")
   - Do say: "AI companion trained on your knowledge to help share it"
   - Be upfront: "This is new technology. We prioritize ethics and control."

### Why This Works at Early Stage
- Builds trust — users feel safe, not exploited
- Protects you legally — clear consent + ownership + non-personhood
- Easy to update later as you grow
- Matches successful platforms (Substack started with simple terms)

### Next Steps
1. Use a template (e.g., from Termly, iubenda, or free ones on GitHub)
2. Customize with the principles above
3. Have a Nigerian lawyer review (important for local compliance)
4. Make it easy to find (footer links: Privacy · Terms · Companion Agreement)

**Bottom line**: At this stage, **strong, simple, consent-focused privacy/terms are your shield**.  
They protect you more than complex legalese — and make users feel respected.

Do this right now — before launch.  
It’s one of the smartest things you can do for Feedple’s future.
